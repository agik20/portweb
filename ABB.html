Throughout the training, I gained in-depth knowledge of control architecture involving both manual (LCS) and automated (DCS) systems. I implemented control logic that allowed dual-mode operation—where LCS could operate the motor in both directions, while DCS was configured to allow forward motion only. I also worked on simulating fault conditions and configuring external event messages to provide feedback and improve system diagnostics, aligning with industrial safety and operational standards.

In addition to control logic, I was trained to set up essential monitoring parameters such as motor speed (RPM), current (A), and control source status (LCS or DCS). These parameters were then displayed through an HMI interface for real-time supervision. This part of the training helped reinforce my understanding of instrumentation systems, parameter feedback loops, and their importance in maintaining process stability.

Built for edge deployment, the system architecture runs on a Jetson-based ReComputer J30 that captures high-resolution frames, processes them through advanced pose and object detection models, and applies smart post-processing logic to extract meaningful evidence. Cropped results are automatically sent to a server, where they're logged into a database and displayed through a live web interface. This end-to-end system is designed for efficiency, automation, and transparency making it an ideal solution for institutions seeking to enhance exam integrity with minimal manual supervision.
